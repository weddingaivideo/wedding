Bride's Wedding Video Checklist: Everything You Need to Capture

The ultimate bride's wedding video checklist. Every must-capture moment from morning prep to last dance, plus tips for getting the best footage.

7 min read

*You've spent months planning every detail of your wedding. The dress, the flowers, the venue, the music. But when it comes to video, most brides realize too late that they didn't plan what to capture.*

This checklist ensures you don't miss a single moment — from the quiet morning with your bridesmaids to the last dance under the stars.

Why a Video Checklist Matters

Your wedding day will be a blur. Between the emotions, the adrenaline, and the endless stream of people wanting your attention, you will not remember half of what happened.

A video checklist ensures that:

  • Your videographer (or whoever is filming) knows exactly what to capture
  • No critical moments are missed
  • You have a plan for footage even without a professional videographer
  • The highlight video or trailer has everything it needs to tell your story

The Ultimate Bride's Video Checklist

Morning Preparation

**Must-Capture:**

  • [ ] The dress hanging in natural light
  • [ ] Close-up of the shoes, jewelry, and accessories
  • [ ] Bride getting hair and makeup done
  • [ ] Mother helping bride into the dress
  • [ ] Bridesmaids seeing the bride for the first time
  • [ ] Bride's reaction seeing herself fully ready
  • [ ] Reading a letter or gift from the partner
  • [ ] Emotional moment with parents (first look with dad)

**Nice-to-Have:**

  • [ ] Bridesmaids getting ready together
  • [ ] Detail shots of the invitation suite
  • [ ] Perfume, lipstick, and personal items
  • [ ] Champagne toast with bridesmaids
  • [ ] Bride writing in her journal or reading vows

**Pro Tip:** Natural light is your best friend for morning prep. Choose a getting-ready location with large windows. The footage will look cinematic without any extra effort.

Pre-Ceremony

**Must-Capture:**

  • [ ] Venue setup and decor (before guests arrive)
  • [ ] Guests arriving and being seated
  • [ ] Groom waiting at the altar
  • [ ] Wedding party lining up for the processional
  • [ ] Flower girl and ring bearer

**Nice-to-Have:**

  • [ ] Aerial/drone footage of the venue
  • [ ] Close-up of ceremony programs
  • [ ] Guests mingling and finding seats
  • [ ] Groom's reaction when music starts

The Ceremony

**Must-Capture:**

  • [ ] Bridal processional (full walk, multiple angles)
  • [ ] Groom's reaction seeing the bride
  • [ ] Father/mother walking bride down the aisle
  • [ ] Officiant's opening words
  • [ ] Exchange of vows (close-up on faces)
  • [ ] Ring exchange
  • [ ] The first kiss
  • [ ] The pronouncement ("I now pronounce you...")
  • [ ] Recessional (walking back up the aisle)
  • [ ] Guest reactions (parents, grandparents, friends)

**Nice-to-Have:**

  • [ ] Readings by family or friends
  • [ ] Unity ceremony (candle, sand, etc.)
  • [ ] Cultural or religious traditions
  • [ ] Musical performances during ceremony
  • [ ] Wide shot of the full ceremony space

**Pro Tip:** Ask your officiant to repeat vows slowly. Many couples rush through vows, and the audio can be hard to capture. A slower pace = better footage.

Post-Ceremony

**Must-Capture:**

  • [ ] The moment immediately after (just married!)
  • [ ] Confetti/petal toss
  • [ ] Couple's private moment together
  • [ ] Family group photos (as video, not just stills)
  • [ ] Wedding party group shots
  • [ ] Couple portraits in the venue's best locations

**Nice-to-Have:**

  • [ ] Behind-the-scenes of the photo session
  • [ ] Candid moments between posed shots
  • [ ] Couple reading private vows (if not done during ceremony)
  • [ ] Sunset/golden hour footage

Reception

**Must-Capture:**

  • [ ] Venue and table setup (before guests sit)
  • [ ] Couple's grand entrance
  • [ ] First dance (complete song)
  • [ ] Parent dances (father-daughter, mother-son)
  • [ ] Toasts and speeches (full speeches)
  • [ ] Cake cutting
  • [ ] Bouquet/garter toss
  • [ ] Guest reactions throughout

**Nice-to-Have:**

  • [ ] Dinner service and food close-ups
  • [ ] Table conversations and laughter
  • [ ] Cocktail hour highlights
  • [ ] Photo booth moments
  • [ ] Children playing/dancing
  • [ ] Grandparents on the dance floor

Party & Dancing

**Must-Capture:**

  • [ ] Dance floor energy (wide and close-up shots)
  • [ ] Key dance moments (choreographed dances, group dances)
  • [ ] The band or DJ performing
  • [ ] Guests having fun
  • [ ] Couple dancing with friends

**Nice-to-Have:**

  • [ ] Dance-off or surprise performances
  • [ ] Sing-along moments
  • [ ] Late-night snack service
  • [ ] Sparkler send-off or exit

The Quiet Moments

These are the moments most checklists miss, but they're often the most powerful in a highlight video:

  • [ ] Couple stealing a private moment
  • [ ] A quiet embrace between the couple
  • [ ] Bride looking at her ring
  • [ ] A stolen glance across the room
  • [ ] End-of-night exhaustion and happiness

What If You Don't Have a Videographer?

No videographer? No problem. Here's how to still capture everything on this checklist.

Assign "Video Buddies"

Designate 2-3 tech-savvy guests to capture key moments:

  • **Person 1**: Ceremony and speeches (stationary, good audio)
  • **Person 2**: Candid moments and guest reactions (roaming)
  • **Person 3**: Details, prep, and behind-the-scenes

Use a Tripod

For the ceremony, set up a phone on a tripod with a clear view. Modern phones capture excellent video. Just make sure it's charged and has storage.

Collect Guest Footage

After the wedding, create a shared folder (Google Drive, Dropbox) and ask guests to upload their photos and videos. You'll be surprised how much great footage guests capture from angles you'd never think of.

Video Checklist by Wedding Style

Indoor Wedding

  • Focus on lighting — ensure ceremony and reception spaces are well-lit for video
  • Capture venue architecture and decor
  • Use window light for prep footage

Outdoor Wedding

  • Plan for weather — have a backup for equipment protection
  • Capture landscape and natural beauty
  • Golden hour footage is especially stunning
  • Wind can affect audio — use a windscreen if possible

Destination Wedding

  • Capture arrival and venue exploration
  • Include local culture and scenery
  • Film welcome events and excursions
  • Document the "destination" aspect — it's part of the story. See our destination wedding video guide.

Small/Intimate Wedding

  • With fewer guests, focus more on emotional close-ups
  • Capture the intimacy and connection
  • Every guest matters — get footage of each person
  • See our small wedding video ideas for more tips.

The Bride's Personal Video Requests

Beyond the standard moments, consider what's personally meaningful to you:

  • [ ] A specific family tradition
  • [ ] A tribute to someone who's passed
  • [ ] A surprise for the groom (or from the groom)
  • [ ] A cultural ceremony element
  • [ ] A specific song or dance
  • [ ] A private message to your future self

Write down your personal must-haves and share them with whoever is capturing video. They can't read your mind.
