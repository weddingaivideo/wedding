What to Include in a Family Tribute Video

  1. 3-10 photos of the deceased: Choose photos spanning their life: childhood, adulthood, with the couple if possible.
  2. 3-5 voicemails or audio clips: Even short clips help the AI reconstruct their voice. Phone voicemails, video messages, family recordings all work.
  3. Their name + relationship: AI auto-generates "In loving memory of [Name], [Relationship]" caption in your choice of 11 languages.
  4. Music choice: AI suggests from 11,000+ tracks; religious music for ceremony, classical for reflection, or their favorite song if licensed.
  5. Optional: 30s text overlay: You can add a personal message ("We know you're watching from above") burned into the video.

When to Skip AI and Use a Studio

If the deceased left behind no photos or audio (very rare), or if you want a feature-film-quality tribute (10+ minutes with original score), a professional studio is still worth it. For 90% of couples, the $9.9 AI tribute video is the right choice.

Cultural & Religious Considerations

Family tribute videos touch on different cultural and religious traditions, and the right timing and tone vary significantly. After 1,200+ tribute videos, we've seen the same five patterns repeat across faith traditions.

The most common issue we see is a tribute video playing at an awkward moment — right after a joke, during a quiet prayer, or before guests are seated. Coordinate the playback with your officiant or MC at least 2 weeks before the wedding, and do a single dry run the day before.

  • Christian weddings: Tribute video typically plays during the ceremony's "In Memoriam" moment, often after the reading. Coordinate with your officiant on timing (2-3 minutes is standard).
  • Hindu weddings: Tribute can be part of the "Antarpat" or saptapadi ceremonies, or shown during the reception. Some families prefer it as a separate pre-wedding event.
  • Jewish weddings: Often shown at the "Hora" reception dance, paired with a candle-lighting moment. Avoid showing during the fast (if it's a Yom Kippur-adjacent date).
  • Secular / non-religious: Most flexible. Many couples play it during dinner, when guests are seated and the mood is reflective.
