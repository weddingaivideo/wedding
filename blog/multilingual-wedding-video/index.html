How Multilingual Wedding Videos Work

  1. Choose your source language: Most couples choose English as source; AI handles translation to any of 11 target languages.
  2. Pick target languages: Select 1-11 languages from EN / ZH / ES / FR / DE / JA / PT / AR / TH / HI / RU. Each language = same video, localized.
  3. AI translates + dubs: AI auto-generates subtitles burned into the video + AI voice dubbing in the target language. Idiom-aware for wedding context.
  4. Style customization per language: Right-to-left languages (AR) auto-adjust subtitle positioning; CJK languages (ZH/JA) use native fonts.
  5. Delivery: one file or per-language: Choose: single video with all subtitles OR separate per-language MP4 files for sharing in family WhatsApp groups.

Best Use Cases

Multilingual wedding videos shine when: one partner speaks English as second language, family abroad will watch the video, vows / toasts include non-English phrases, or you want to share on multilingual social media. For monolingual ceremonies, skip this service.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Multilingual wedding videos succeed when you plan ahead. After producing over 4,000 multilingual films for couples worldwide, we've seen the same three mistakes recur. The good news: each one is easy to avoid with a 10-minute pre-production check.

Skipping these checks doesn't just hurt quality — it can mean redoing the entire video (an extra 9 minutes and one of your two free revisions) or, worse, an embarrassing mistranslation that surfaces at the most inopportune moment during the ceremony.

  • Skipping a native-speaker review: AI translation is 90%+ accurate but wedding vows include idioms and inside jokes. Always have a native-speaking family member review the final video before sharing with the wedding group.
  • Translating in the wrong direction: Most couples think English-to-target. But if your spouse's family speaks Hindi and you don't, ask for Hindi-to-English subtitles so YOU can follow the toasts live at the reception.
  • Choosing too many languages: 11 languages is the cap, but quality drops if you spread budget across all 11. Pick the 3-4 most important family languages first; add more only if budget allows.
  • Forgetting audio rights: If your ceremony music is licensed (e.g. a Bruno Mars song), dubbing in another language may require re-licensing. AI video generation uses royalty-free music by default to avoid this.
