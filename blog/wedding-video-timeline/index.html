How to Create a Wedding Video Timeline: When to Film What

Plan your wedding video timeline with our expert guide. Know exactly what to film and when for a complete wedding film. Download our free template!

9 min read · Updated 2026-08-04

You've spent months planning every detail of your wedding day — the flowers, the food, the first dance. But have you planned what your videographer should film and when? A well-structured wedding video timeline ensures you capture every meaningful moment without the chaos of last-minute decisions. Whether you're hiring a professional or filming with friends and phones, a timeline is the difference between a complete wedding film and a collection of random clips.

Why You Need a Wedding Video Timeline

A wedding day moves fast. Between hair appointments, photo sessions, and cocktail hour, there are dozens of moments worth capturing — and dozens of ways to miss them. Without a timeline, you'll end up with beautiful footage of the reception but nothing from the morning preparations. Or you'll capture the ceremony perfectly but miss the quiet moment between the couple right before they walked down the aisle.

The Statistics Tell the Story

The Knot's Real Weddings Study, the average wedding lasts 5-6 hours (ceremony through reception), but the full wedding day spans 12-16 hours from first hair appointment to last dance. A timeline ensures your video captures the complete story, not just the public events.

What a Timeline Prevents

  • Missing key momentsbecause nobody knew they were supposed to be filmed
  • Overlapping prioritieswhere the videographer is in one room when something important happens in another
  • Rushed footagebecause there wasn't enough time allocated for specific shots
  • Gaps in the storywhere the video jumps from one event to the next without context

First Look (Optional — 1-2 Hours Before Ceremony)

If you're doing a first look, this is one of the most emotionally powerful segments of your wedding video:

  • The approach— groom waiting, anticipation building
  • The reveal— the tap on the shoulder, the turn, the reaction
  • The couple's private moment— 5-10 minutes alone together
  • Couple portraits— natural, relaxed footage while the emotion is fresh

First looks produce incredible video content because the couple is relaxed, the lighting is usually great, and the emotions are raw and unfiltered.

Ceremony (30-60 Minutes)

The ceremony is the heart of your wedding video. Here's what to capture:

Pre-Ceremony (15-30 minutes before)

  • Venue details — flowers, arch, aisle decorations
  • Guests arriving and finding seats
  • Officiant preparing

The Processional

  • Groom's reaction watching the bridal party enter
  • Flower girl and ring bearer (always entertaining)
  • Parents being seated
  • The bride's entrance— this is the most important single shot of the entire day

The Ceremony

  • Wide shots of the full ceremony
  • Close-ups during vows — both faces
  • Ring exchange
  • The first kiss
  • The recessional — the couple walking back up the aisle

Pro tip:Allocate at least two camera angles for the ceremony if possible. One wide and one tight. If you're relying on guest recordings, assign specific people to specific angles. Our guide onwhat to film at a weddingbreaks this down further.

Post-Ceremony (30-60 Minutes)

  • Candid congratulations— hugs, tears, laughter as guests greet the couple
  • Couple portraits— golden hour shots if timing allows
  • Family formal photos— these often have great candid moments between posed shots
  • Bridal party photos— fun, relaxed group content

Cocktail Hour (45-60 Minutes)

  • Guest interactions and mingling
  • Venue ambiance shots
  • Signature drinks being prepared
  • Detail shots of centerpieces, table settings, favors
  • Kids playing, candid guest moments

Adapting the Timeline for Different Wedding Styles

Not every wedding follows the traditional format. Here's how to adjust your video timeline:

Elopement Video Timeline

Elopements are simpler but still need planning:

  1. Preparation— getting ready at the hotel or Airbnb (30-45 min)
  2. Journey to location— the drive or hike (15-30 min)
  3. The ceremony— intimate vows in a stunning location (15-20 min)
  4. Celebration— champagne toast, exploring the location together (1-2 hours)
  5. Golden hour portraits— the money shots (30-60 min)

Micro-Wedding Timeline

Micro-weddings (under 30 guests) often compress the timeline:

  • Shorter getting-ready period (1-2 hours)
  • More intimate ceremony (15-20 minutes)
  • Combined cocktail hour and reception
  • More candid time with each guest

Working With Your Videographer on the Timeline

Your timeline is a communication tool between you and your videographer. Here's how to make it work:

Share It Early

Send your timeline to your videographer at least 2-4 weeks before the wedding. This gives them time to scout locations, plan equipment, and flag potential issues.

Include Contact Information

Your timeline should list key people and their phone numbers — the wedding planner, maid of best man, venue coordinator, and photographer. When the videographer needs to find someone quickly, they shouldn't have to guess.

Build in Buffer Time

Everything at a wedding takes longer than planned. Build 15-20 minute buffers between major events. This prevents the cascade effect where one delay pushes everything back, and the videographer has to choose between filming the end of cocktail hour or the start of the first dance.

Prioritize Your Must-Haves

Create a ranked list of your top 10 must-have video moments. Share this with your videographer so they know where to position themselves when conflicts arise. Your list might look like:

  1. Bride's entrance / groom's reaction
  2. Vows
  3. First kiss
  4. First dance
  5. Father-daughter dance
  6. Toasts
  7. Cake cutting
  8. Bouquet toss
  9. Dance floor highlights
  10. Send-off

DIY Wedding Video Timeline Tips

If you're filming your wedding with phones and consumer cameras instead of a professional videographer, your timeline becomes even more important:

Assign Filming Duties

Don't rely on one person to film everything. Assign 3-5 guests to specific moments:

  • Person A: Ceremony (stationary, wide angle)
  • Person B: Ceremony (mobile, close-ups)
  • Person C: Getting ready + candid moments
  • Person D: Reception events
  • Person E: Dance floor and candid guest footage

Create a Shot List

Give each person a printed shot list with their specific assignments. Include the time, location, and exact shots they need. Vague instructions like "film the wedding" produce vague footage.

Do a Test Run

If possible, visit the venue before the wedding and test camera positions. Check lighting at the same time of day as your ceremony. Identify where power outlets are for charging. Note where the sun will be during outdoor events.

Common Timeline Mistakes to Avoid

After helping hundreds of couples plan their wedding videos, we've seen these timeline mistakes repeatedly:

  • Not allocating enough time for getting ready: The morning is full of emotional, intimate moments. Rushing through it means losing some of the best content.
  • Scheduling portraits during golden hour AND cocktail hour: Pick one. If you're doing portraits during golden hour, your videographer can't also capture cocktail hour.
  • Forgetting the in-between moments: The walk between ceremony and reception, the quiet moment in the hallway, the late-night conversation at a table. These unscripted moments often become the most treasured footage.
  • No plan for the end of the night: The send-off is often chaotic and unplanned. Decide in advance what it looks like and make sure someone is filming it.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I create my wedding video timeline?

Create your timeline 4-6 weeks before the wedding. Share it with your videographer 2-4 weeks before the day. Finalize any last-minute changes 3-5 days before the event.

What if my wedding doesn't follow a traditional format?

Every wedding is unique. Use the template above as a starting point, then adjust for your specific events, cultural traditions, and personal priorities. The key principles — allocate time, assign priorities, and build in buffers — apply to any format.

How long should each part of the wedding video be?

A typical highlight reel runs 5-10 minutes and covers the entire day. A full-length film runs 45-90 minutes. Most videographers create both. For DIY footage, aim to capture at least 2-3 minutes of usable content per major event.

Can WeddingAI.video work with footage filmed on phones?

AI Video Generatoris designed to work with footage from any source — professional cameras, phones, GoPros, and guest recordings. The AI standardizes quality and combines clips into a cohesive film.

Should I include quiet moments in my timeline?

Absolutely. The quiet moments — a private conversation before the ceremony, a parent watching from across the room, a stolen kiss in a hallway — are often the most emotionally powerful footage in a wedding video. Build in 10-15 minutes of "unstructured" filming time for these spontaneous moments.
