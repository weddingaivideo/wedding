## Step 1: Plan Your Video Before You Start

### Decide on Your Video Type

Before you touch a single photo or video clip, decide what kind of wedding video you want to create:

- **Highlight reel** (2–5 minutes) — A cinematic montage of your best moments set to music. Perfect for social media and quick sharing. This is the most popular format and the easiest to create.
- **Feature film** (15–60 minutes) — A comprehensive documentary-style video covering the entire day, including speeches, ceremonies, and candid moments.
- **Same-day edit** (3–5 minutes) — A quick video shown at the reception, created from photos and clips taken earlier that day.
- **Thank-you video** (1–2 minutes) — A short video sent to guests after the wedding, expressing gratitude.
- **Wedding trailer** (1–2 minutes) — A teaser-style video that builds excitement, like a movie trailer for your wedding.

For most couples, a highlight reel is the perfect starting point. It's manageable in scope, shareable in length, and endlessly rewatchable. If you want something more elaborate, you can always create multiple videos for different purposes.

### Gather Your Materials

You'll need:

- **Photos** — 30–80 of your best wedding photos (more on selection in Step 2)
- **Video clips** (optional) — Raw footage from your videographer, phone recordings, or drone footage
- **Music** — 1–3 songs that capture the mood of your wedding (we'll cover music selection in detail below)
- **Text** — Names, dates, vows, quotes, or any text you want to include

### Choose Your Tool

Your choice of tool will significantly impact the ease and quality of your final video:

- **AI-powered platforms** (like [WeddingAI Video](/)) — Fastest and easiest. Upload photos, choose a style, and get a professional video in minutes.
- **Mobile apps** — Apps like CapCut, InShot, or iMovie offer decent editing on your phone with moderate learning curves.
- **Desktop software** — Adobe Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro, or DaVinci Resolve offer maximum control but require significant time and skill.

For this tutorial, we'll cover the process for all approaches, with a focus on making it accessible for beginners.

## Step 2: Select and Organize Your Photos

### How Many Photos Do You Need?

The ideal number depends on your target video length:

| Video Length | Recommended Photos |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 1 minute | 15–25 photos |
| 2 minutes | 30–50 photos |
| 3–5 minutes | 50–80 photos |
| 5+ minutes | 80–150 photos |

A good rule of thumb: each photo appears on screen for approximately 3–4 seconds when synced to music at a natural pace.

### Which Photos Work Best?

Not all photos are created equal when it comes to video. Here's what to prioritize:

**High-resolution images** — Blurry or pixelated photos will look even worse in video. Aim for images that are at least 1920×1080 pixels.

**Variety of shots** — Include a mix of:
- Wide shots (venue, landscape, group photos)
- Medium shots (couple portraits, ceremony moments)
- Close-ups (rings, flowers, expressions, details)
- Candid moments (laughter, tears, dance floor)

**Emotional range** — The best wedding videos tell an emotional story. Include photos that capture:
- Anticipation (getting ready)
- Joy (ceremony, first kiss)
- Tenderness (first dance, quiet moments)
- Celebration (reception, dancing, toasts)

**Horizontal orientation** — Video screens are horizontal. While vertical photos can work, horizontal photos generally look better. If you have important vertical photos, consider using them with a blurred background fill.

For a detailed checklist of what photos to gather, see our guide on [what photos you need for a wedding video](/wedding-video-photo-checklist).

### Organize Your Selection

Create folders organized by the timeline of your day:

1. **Getting Ready** — Bridal prep, groom prep, details
2. **Ceremony** — Processional, vows, ring exchange, first kiss
3. **Portraits** — Couple photos, family photos, bridal party
4. **Reception** — Entrance, first dance, toasts, cake cutting
5. **Celebration** — Dancing, candid moments, send-off

This organization will make the editing process much smoother, whether you're using AI or traditional software.

## Step 3: Choose Your Music

Music is the soul of your wedding video. The right song can elevate simple photos into a cinematic experience, while the wrong one can make even the best footage feel flat.

### Music Selection Tips

- **Match the mood** — A romantic ballad for tender moments, an upbeat track for celebration scenes.
- **Consider pacing** — Songs with a clear build-up work beautifully for wedding videos, starting gentle and crescendoing to a powerful finish.
- **Check licensing** — If you plan to share your video online (especially on YouTube or Instagram), make sure your music is properly licensed. Using copyrighted music can result in your video being muted or taken down.
- **Think about lyrics** — Instrumental music is versatile and timeless. If you choose a song with lyrics, make sure the words complement your story.

If you prefer more hands-on control:

2. **Arrange on timeline** in your preferred order.
3. **Set duration** for each photo (typically 3–4 seconds each).
4. **Add transitions** — Simple crossfades work best for wedding videos.
5. **Add music** — Import your chosen track and align it with the visuals.
6. **Add text overlays** — Names, dates, and captions.
7. **Add effects** — Subtle zoom, pan, or [slow motion effects](/services/video-enhancement/slow-motion/) where appropriate.

**Total time: 1–3 hours.**

### Option C: Using Desktop Software

For maximum creative control:

1. **Create a new project** with settings matching your output needs (1080p at 30fps is standard).
2. **Import all media** — Photos, video clips, music, and any additional assets.
3. **Build your timeline** — Arrange clips and photos in narrative order.
4. **Add keyframes** — Create smooth pan and zoom animations on photos (the "Ken Burns effect").
5. **Color grade** — Apply a consistent color treatment across all images.
6. **Add transitions** — Crossfades, dissolves, and occasional creative transitions.
7. **Mix audio** — Balance music levels, add ambient audio from video clips if applicable.
8. **Add titles and credits** — Opening titles, lower thirds, and closing credits.
9. **Review multiple times** — Check for timing, pacing, and emotional flow.
10. **Export** — Render in high quality for your final delivery format.

## Step 6: Add Captions and Text

Well-designed text enhances your video without distracting from the visuals. Key moments for text include:

- **Opening** — Your names and wedding date
- **Venue identification** — Location name and city
- **Chapter markers** — "Getting Ready," "The Ceremony," "The Reception"
- **Quotes** — Meaningful lines from your vows or a favorite poem
- **Closing** — "Thank you" message or a dedication

## Step 7: Review and Refine

Before finalizing your video, watch it through several times with fresh eyes:

- **First viewing** — Watch the entire video without pausing. Does it flow? Does it tell a story?
- **Second viewing** — Check technical details. Are photos high quality? Are transitions smooth?
- **Third viewing** — Watch with sound. Does the music sync well? Are audio levels balanced?
- **Get feedback** — Share a draft with your partner, a trusted friend, or family member.

Common issues to watch for:
- Photos that appear blurry or pixelated
- Transitions that feel jarring or too frequent
- Music that doesn't match the emotional tone
- Text that's too small, too fast, or poorly positioned
- Pacing that feels too rushed or too slow

## Step 8: Export and Share

### Export Settings

- **Resolution** — 1080p (Full HD) is standard for sharing. 4K is ideal for archival purposes.
- **Format** — MP4 with H.264 encoding is universally compatible.
- **Aspect ratio** — 16:9 for standard viewing, 9:16 for Instagram/TikTok stories.

### Where to Share

- **Social media** — Instagram, Facebook, TikTok (consider creating shortened versions for each platform)
- **Video platforms** — YouTube or Vimeo for easy sharing with a link
- **Direct sharing** — Email, messaging apps, or cloud storage links
- **Wedding website** — Embed your video on your wedding website for guests
- **Physical media** — USB drives or DVDs for grandparents and family members who prefer tangible keepsakes

## Tips for a Professional-Looking Result

### Do:

- Start and end with your strongest images
- Use high-resolution photos exclusively
- Maintain consistent color treatment throughout
- Match video pacing to music tempo
- Include a variety of shot types
- Keep it concise — shorter is almost always better

### Don't:

- Overuse transitions or effects
- Include too many similar-looking photos
- Use low-quality or blurry images
- Choose music with distracting lyrics
- Make the video longer than the content warrants
- Forget to include candid and emotional moments
