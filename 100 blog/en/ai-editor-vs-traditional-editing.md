## The Traditional Editing Workflow

### What Does Traditional Editing Involve?

Traditional video editing is a multi-step, labor-intensive process. Even a simple 3-minute wedding highlight reel can take anywhere from 8 to 20 hours of work. Here's what a typical workflow looks like:

1. **Importing and organizing footage** — Sorting through hours of raw video and hundreds of photos to select the best material.
2. **Creating a rough cut** — Laying out clips in chronological order on a timeline.
3. **Trimming and refining** — Cutting out awkward moments, adjusting pacing, and ensuring smooth transitions.
4. **Color grading** — Adjusting the color, contrast, and tone to create a consistent visual style.
5. **Adding music and audio** — Syncing background music, adjusting audio levels, and possibly adding voiceover.
6. **Adding titles and effects** — Creating text overlays, transitions, and special effects.
7. **Exporting and rendering** — Converting the final project into a shareable video format.

Each of these steps requires technical knowledge, creative judgment, and patience.

### The Software Learning Curve

Professional editing software is designed for professionals. Here's a realistic look at what it takes to learn each major platform:

- **Adobe Premiere Pro** — Industry standard with a subscription model ($20.99/month). Beginners typically need 40–80 hours of practice before producing polished results.
- **Final Cut Pro** — Apple's professional editor ($299.99 one-time). Slightly more intuitive but still requires 30–60 hours of learning.
- **DaVinci Resolve** — Free version available, but the professional features that make it worthwhile require the paid version ($295). Famous for its color grading capabilities but notoriously complex.

For most couples, the question isn't whether these tools *can* produce amazing results — they absolutely can. The question is whether investing dozens of hours learning software makes sense when you could be enjoying married life.

### The Cost Factor

Beyond the software itself, traditional editing carries hidden costs:

- **Hardware requirements** — Professional editing software demands a powerful computer with dedicated graphics, at least 16GB of RAM, and fast storage. If your laptop can't handle it, you're looking at a $1,500–$3,000 upgrade.
- **Stock music licenses** — Professional-quality wedding music tracks cost $15–$50 each.
- **Plugin costs** — Color grading LUTs, transition packs, and effect plugins can add $100–$500 to your project.
- **Your time** — Even at minimum wage, 20 hours of editing represents hundreds of dollars in opportunity cost.

Or, you could hire a professional editor — which typically costs $500–$2,000 for a wedding highlight video, with turnaround times of 4–12 weeks.

To be fair, AI editing isn't perfect for every situation:

- **Audio mixing** — If your video includes speech, interviews, or complex audio, traditional editing provides more precise audio control.
- **Very long videos** — AI is optimized for highlight reels and shorter formats. Feature-length wedding films may still benefit from traditional editing.
