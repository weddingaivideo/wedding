## What Is a Wedding Trailer?

A wedding trailer is a short, cinematic teaser video — typically 60-90 seconds — that captures the essence of your wedding day. Think of it as the highlight reel's edgier, more dramatic cousin.

While a [highlight video](/wedding-highlight-video.md) tells the full story in 4-6 minutes, a trailer distills everything into a rapid-fire emotional punch. It's designed for one purpose: to make people *feel something* instantly.

### Wedding Trailer vs. Highlight Video

- **Trailer**: 60-90 seconds, high energy, teaser-style, perfect for social media
- **Highlight Video**: 3-7 minutes, narrative arc, complete story, great for rewatching

Many couples create both — a trailer for immediate sharing, and a highlight video for the full experience.

## The Anatomy of a Great Wedding Trailer

Not all trailers are created equal. Here's what separates a scroll-stopping trailer from a forgettable slideshow.

### The Hook (First 3 Seconds)

You have three seconds to grab attention. The best trailers open with one of these:

- A dramatic close-up (hands, rings, eyes)
- A powerful audio clip (a vow excerpt, a laugh, a cheer)
- A stunning wide shot (the venue, the landscape)
- A text card with a provocative statement

### The Build (3-30 Seconds)

Quick cuts that establish the setting and build anticipation. Getting ready shots, venue details, guests arriving. The pace should be fast — each shot lasting 1-3 seconds.

### The Emotional Core (30-60 Seconds)

This is where the magic happens. Ceremony highlights. Vow excerpts. The first kiss. Emotional reactions from family. Music swells. Pace slows slightly to let moments breathe.

### The Celebration (60-80 Seconds)

Energy ramps back up. Dancing, laughing, partying. Fast cuts synchronized to the music's beat. Pure joy.

### The Close (Final 10 Seconds)

A final, quiet moment. A last look. A kiss under stars. Title card with your names and date. Music fades.

## Step-by-Step: How to Make a Wedding Trailer

### Step 1: Gather Your Footage

Collect all video clips from your videographer, guests' phones, and any drone footage. You'll want 20-50 clips minimum for a 60-90 second trailer.

**If you have neither:**
Some couples are now creating trailers from a combination of:

- Venue photos
- Getting-ready photos taken by friends
- Stock-style footage of the location

### Step 2: Choose Your Music

Music is everything. A wedding trailer without the right song is like a horror movie without a soundtrack — technically complete, but emotionally empty.

**Tips for music selection:**

- **Match the energy arc**: Start soft, build to a crescendo, then bring it home
- **Use trending audio wisely**: If a song is trending on TikTok, it can boost visibility — but make sure it fits your vibe
- **Consider instrumental**: Lyrics can distract; instrumental tracks let the visuals shine
- **Check licensing**: If you're posting on social media, make sure the music is licensed or use royalty-free options

**Popular wedding trailer music styles in 2026:**
- Cinematic orchestral builds
- Acoustic/indie emotional ballads
- Modern classical piano
- Atmospheric electronic ambient

### Step 3: Edit for Impact

**Pacing is king.** Here's a rough guide for a 75-second trailer:

| Section | Duration | Shot Length | Energy |
|---------|----------|-------------|--------|
| Hook | 0-3s | 1-2s | Medium |
| Build | 3-25s | 1-3s | Rising |
| Emotional Core | 25-55s | 2-5s | Peak |
| Celebration | 55-70s | 1-2s | High |
| Close | 70-75s | 3-5s | Gentle |

**Editing tips:**
- Cut on the beat. Every transition should sync with the music
- Use speed ramps — normal speed to slow motion on emotional moments
- Layer ambient audio (cheers, laughter, applause) under the music
- Keep it tight. Every second must earn its place

### Step 4: Add Text and Titles

Minimal text, maximum impact. You typically need:
- Your names (opening or closing)
- Date and location (subtle, not dominant)
- Optional: a meaningful quote or vow excerpt

### Step 5: Export and Optimize

Export in multiple formats:
- **16:9 (horizontal)**: YouTube, TV viewing
- **9:16 (vertical)**: Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts
- **1:1 (square)**: Instagram feed, Facebook

## Common Wedding Trailer Mistakes

### Making It Too Long

A trailer is a teaser. If it's over 90 seconds, it's not a trailer — it's a short highlight video. Keep it tight.

### Using Generic Music

The music that came free with your editing software is not the right choice. Invest time in finding the perfect track.

### Too Many Slow-Motion Shots

Slow motion is powerful, but a trailer full of slow-mo feels like a perfume ad. Use it for 2-3 key moments only. Learn more about [effective slow-motion use](/services/video-enhancement/slow-motion/).

### Ignoring Audio Design

Don't just slap music over the video. Layer in ambient sounds — the crowd cheering, the waves crashing, the laughter. Audio design is what separates amateur from professional.

### Not Having a Clear Structure

A trailer needs a beginning, middle, and end. Random pretty shots strung together is not a trailer — it's a slideshow.
