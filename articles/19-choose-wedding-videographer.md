# How to Choose a Wedding Videographer: 15 Questions to Ask Before Booking

<!-- Image 1: Bride and groom meeting with a videographer, reviewing portfolio on a tablet. Alt text: "how to choose wedding videographer reviewing portfolio" -->

Choosing a wedding videographer is one of the highest-stakes vendor decisions you'll make. Unlike flowers that wilt or cake that gets eaten, your wedding video is forever. Pick the right videographer and you'll have a film that makes you cry happy tears for decades. Pick the wrong one and you'll have an expensive reminder of what could have been.

The challenge is that every videographer looks good in their portfolio. The polished highlight reels on their website represent their absolute best work — curated from hundreds of weddings. What you need to know is what your specific wedding video will look like, and that requires asking the right questions.

Here are the 15 essential questions to ask before booking a wedding videographer, organized by the stages of the hiring process.

## Before You Start Looking: Know What You Want

Before reaching out to any videographer, get clear on three things:

1. **Your budget**: Wedding videography in the US ranges from $1,000 to $10,000+. Know your number before you start.
2. **Your style preference**: Watch 10-15 wedding videos online and note what you like. Cinematic? Documentary? Fun and energetic? Romantic and slow?
3. **Your must-haves**: Do you need a full ceremony recording? Social media clips? A highlight reel? Multiple cameras?

Having these answers ready will make your conversations with videographers more productive and help you compare options fairly.

## The 15 Essential Questions

### About Their Work

**1. "Can I see a full wedding video, not just a highlight reel?"**

This is the single most important question. Highlight reels are curated showcases of the best 3-5 minutes from an entire wedding. They tell you almost nothing about the videographer's ability to edit a complete story. A full wedding video — ideally from a wedding similar to yours in size, venue type, and season — shows you the real quality of their work.

What to look for: consistent quality throughout, good audio during vows and speeches, smooth transitions, and a narrative that holds your attention beyond the first 5 minutes.

**2. "How would you describe your editing style?"**

Videographers typically fall into one of these categories:

| Style | Description | Best For |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| Cinematic | Movie-like, dramatic, music-driven | Couples who want a "film" feel |
| Documentary | Real-time, minimal editing, natural | Couples who want authenticity |
| Journalistic | Story-focused, chronological, balanced | Couples who want a complete record |
| Music video | High-energy, quick cuts, trending audio | Couples who want social media content |

There's no right answer — just make sure their style matches your vision. If you want a romantic, slow-motion-heavy cinematic film and they specialize in fast-paced documentary editing, it's not a good fit regardless of their talent.

**3. "Who will actually shoot and edit my wedding?"**

Some videography companies employ multiple shooters and editors. The person whose work you fell in love with might not be the person who shows up on your wedding day. Get a commitment in writing about who will handle your specific wedding.

This is especially important for larger companies. Ask to see work from the specific shooter/editor who would be assigned to you, not just the company's best-of reel.

**4. "How many weddings do you shoot per year?"**

This question reveals their experience level and their availability. According to [WeddingWire's vendor survey](https://www.weddingwire.com/cost), the average full-time wedding videographer shoots 30-40 weddings per year. A videographer who shoots 80+ weddings per year is experienced but potentially stretched thin. One who shoots 15-25 weddings per year likely gives each couple more personal attention.

There's no magic number, but be cautious of extremes — too many weddings means potential burnout and rushed editing; too few might mean limited experience.

### About Logistics

**5. "What's included in your standard package?"**

Get specific deliverables in writing:

- Hours of coverage
- Number of videographers
- Length of final edited video(s)
- Raw footage delivery (yes/no)
- Format and resolution of deliverables
- Social media clips (yes/no)
- Drone footage (yes/no)

Packages vary wildly between videographers. Two similarly priced packages might deliver completely different products.

**6. "How many hours of coverage does the package include, and what's the overtime rate?"**

Standard coverage is 8-10 hours. If your wedding timeline extends beyond the contracted hours, you need to know the overtime rate upfront. Some videographers charge $200-$500 per additional hour. Others won't extend at all, meaning the coverage simply ends.

**7. "What happens if you're sick or unable to attend?"**

Every professional videographer should have a backup plan. Ask whether they have a network of trusted colleagues who can step in, and whether you'd have approval over the substitute. If they're a solo operator with no backup plan, that's a risk you should be aware of.

**8. "Do you carry backup equipment?"**

Cameras fail. Cards corrupt. Batteries die. A professional videographer should carry backup camera bodies, lenses, memory cards, batteries, and audio equipment. One camera and one card is not professional — it's a gamble.

### About the Final Product

**9. "What's your turnaround time?"**

Industry standard is 8-16 weeks for the final edited video. Some videographers deliver faster; others take 6+ months. Get a specific timeline in your contract, and ask about interim deliverables — many couples want at least a highlight reel or social media clip within the first few weeks.

If you need faster turnaround, consider services that use [AI-assisted editing](/blog/ai-vs-human-wedding-editing/) to deliver results in days rather than months.

**10. "What format will my video be delivered in?"**

Understanding [wedding video formats](/blog/wedding-video-formats/) helps you ask the right questions. At minimum, you want:

- Final video in MP4 (H.264 or H.265) at 4K resolution
- Raw footage delivered separately on a hard drive or cloud download
- Social media-optimized versions if included in the package

**11. "Will I receive the raw, unedited footage?"**

This is non-negotiable for many couples, and for good reason. Raw footage gives you the flexibility to create new edits, use clips for other projects, or have a different editor work with your material in the future. Some videographers include raw footage; others charge extra. Either way, make sure it's addressed in your contract.

If you're interested in creating additional content from your footage — like [social media clips](/blog/wedding-video-social-media/) or [family tribute videos](/services/absence-compensation/family-tribute/) — having the raw footage is essential.

**12. "How many revision rounds are included?"**

Most videographers include 1-2 revision rounds in their package. This means you can request changes to the edit — adjusting the music, removing a scene, changing the pacing — before the final delivery. Additional revisions typically cost $100-$300 per round.

### About Business Practices

**13. "Can I see reviews or speak with past clients?"**

Online reviews are a start, but speaking directly with a past client gives you unfiltered insight. Ask the videographer for 2-3 references from weddings similar to yours. Questions to ask references: Were they responsive? Did they deliver on time? Were there any surprises? Would you hire them again?

**14. "What's your cancellation and refund policy?"**

Life happens. Weddings get postponed, relationships end, emergencies arise. Understand the cancellation policy before you sign. Most videographers retain a non-refundable deposit (typically 25-50% of the total), with the balance due before or on the wedding day.

**15. "Do you have liability insurance?"**

This is a question most couples forget to ask. Professional videographers should carry liability insurance, which protects you if they cause damage at the venue or if their equipment injures a guest. Many venues require vendors to carry insurance — check with yours.

## Red Flags to Watch For

During your search, be cautious of these warning signs:

- **No full wedding videos available**: Only showing highlight reels is a sign they may not be able to sustain quality over a full edit
- **Vague pricing**: If they won't give you a clear quote with specific deliverables, expect hidden costs
- **No contract**: A professional videographer always uses a written contract
- **No backup equipment or backup plan**: This is a professionalism issue, not a budget issue
- **Pressure to book immediately**: "I have another couple interested in your date" is sometimes legitimate, but high-pressure tactics are a red flag
- **Unwillingness to meet or video chat**: You're trusting this person with one of the most important days of your life — a face-to-face conversation is reasonable

## Making Your Final Decision

After meeting with videographers and asking these questions, compare your options using this framework:

1. **Portfolio quality**: Do you love their actual work (not just their highlights)?
2. **Style fit**: Does their natural style match your vision?
3. **Personality fit**: Do you enjoy being around them? They'll be with you all day.
4. **Value**: Does the package deliver what you need at a price you can afford?
5. **Professionalism**: Are they responsive, organized, and transparent?
6. **Reviews**: Do past clients speak positively about the experience?

Trust your gut. If something feels off during the consultation, it'll feel worse on your wedding day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How far in advance should I book a wedding videographer?
Book 9-12 months before your wedding date. Popular videographers fill their schedules quickly, especially during peak wedding season (May-October). If you're getting married in less than 6 months, start reaching out immediately — you'll have fewer options but good videographers can still be found.

### How much should I spend on a wedding videographer?
Budget 10-15% of your total wedding budget for videography. In the US, this typically means $1,500-$5,000 for professional coverage. If that's beyond your budget, consider AI-powered alternatives that deliver professional results from raw footage at significantly lower costs.

### Is it worth hiring a separate videographer and photographer?
Yes, if your budget allows. They're different disciplines that require different skills and equipment. A photographer who also offers video is often better at one than the other. Dedicated videographers bring specialized equipment, audio gear, and editing expertise that dual-discipline vendors may lack.

### What if I can't afford a professional videographer?
You have several options: ask a tech-savvy friend to capture key moments, set up stationary cameras at the ceremony and reception, ask guests to shoot clips on their phones, or use an AI-powered editing service to create a polished video from whatever footage you collect. ### Should I tip my wedding videographer?
Tipping isn't required but is appreciated, especially if they've gone above and beyond. A tip of $50-$200 per videographer is customary. If the owner of the company is shooting your wedding, tipping is optional but welcome.
