Wedding Guest Count Calculator Guide

The number you invite and the number you feed are different numbers. Most budget blowups start with treating them as the same.

Last reviewed · 2026-11-15

Open the free tool →

Quick reference

Local guestsCommonly 80–90% accept when travel is not required
Out of townCommonly 60–75%, lower when lodging is expensive
DestinationOften 35–55% of the initial list
BufferKeep 5% headroom before quoting a final headcount

How it works

Split the list into groups before applying a rate: immediate family, local friends, out-of-town friends, colleagues, and plus-ones. A blended 75% applied to everyone overestimates a destination wedding and underestimates a local one. Enter each group separately and the estimate becomes useful enough to price a venue.

Regional differences

In the US it is normal to invite colleagues and give most single guests a plus-one, which inflates the list. UK and European weddings more often name each guest explicitly and split day guests from evening guests, so the catering count and the evening count are two separate figures. In parts of South Asia the invitation list is community-wide and a 500-person event is not unusual.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not use the estimate as a booking number. Venues and caterers price on a contracted minimum and a final guaranteed count. Lock the venue at a capacity that fits your upper bound, then give the caterer the confirmed number only after RSVPs close, adding the buffer you kept.

Quick answers

What percentage of wedding guests actually attend?

A common planning range is 75–85% overall, but it varies sharply by travel distance, season and whether the date falls on a holiday weekend.

Should we invite children?

Decide before sending invitations and state it clearly on the invitation or wedding website. Ambiguity here produces the largest source of unexpected headcount.

When is the final headcount due?

Caterers typically require the guaranteed number 7 to 14 days before the event. Set your RSVP deadline at least two weeks earlier.
