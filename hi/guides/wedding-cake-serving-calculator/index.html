Wedding Cake Servings Guide

Cake slices are cut smaller than birthday slices. A standard wedding portion is about 1 by 2 inches, which is why a three-tier cake can serve far more people than it looks.

Last reviewed · 2026-11-15

Quick reference

PortionA typical wedding slice is roughly 1 × 2 × 4 inches
6-inch tierCommonly serves about 12 wedding portions
8-inch tierCommonly serves about 24 wedding portions
10-inch tierCommonly serves about 38 wedding portions

How it works

Add the tier capacities, then subtract a little for the irregular top tier and any fondant-heavy decoration that slows cutting. If the cake is dessert, plan one slice per confirmed guest. If it is served after a full meal or alongside a dessert table, plan for 70–80% of guests and tell the caterer how to cut it.

Regional differences

In the US a tall multi-tier display cake is common and caterers often charge a per-slice cutting fee, so a small display cake plus kitchen sheet cake is a frequent cost-saving choice. UK weddings traditionally have a dense fruit cake covered in marzipan and icing, cut by the couple and often served with cheese later; a separate sponge tier is common now. In parts of Europe a croquembouche or a tower of pastries replaces the tiered cake entirely.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not size the cake from the invitation count. Use the guaranteed headcount, and confirm whether the venue's cutting fee applies to cake brought from outside. Also ask who saves the top tier and how it is packed — a frozen tier needs a box and a named person, and it is often the detail nobody assigns.

Quick answers

How many does a three-tier wedding cake serve?

A 6/8/10-inch combination is commonly quoted at around 70–75 wedding-size portions, depending on cutting style and decoration.

Is a sheet cake cheaper?

Usually yes, especially when a caterer charges a per-slice cutting fee. A small display cake for photos plus sheet cake in the kitchen is a common compromise.

Do we need a slice for every guest?

Only if cake is the sole dessert. With a dessert table or a full meal, planning for 70–80% of confirmed guests is usually enough.
