How to Write a Wedding Speech

A wedding speech has one job: make the couple feel seen in front of their guests. Every story should serve that, and every story should end kindly.

Last reviewed · 2026-11-15

Open the free tool →

Quick reference

LengthTwo to four minutes; roughly 300–500 spoken words
OpeningIntroduce yourself and your relationship to the couple
BodyOne story with a point, then one quality you admire
CloseA toast; invite the room to raise a glass

How it works

Use a three-part shape: a short opening, one story that shows rather than tells, and a toast. Write the story as a scene with a beginning and a punchline that lands on the couple, not at their expense. Then cut a third of the draft — almost every first draft is too long and too heavy on in-jokes.

Regional differences

The UK and Ireland typically schedule best man, father of the bride and groom speeches during or after the meal, and the best man speech is expected to be funny. US weddings often spread short toasts across the rehearsal dinner and reception, with a lighter tone. In many European and Asian weddings the parents or elders speak first, and a formal toast order may be set by the family.

Mistakes to avoid

Avoid exes, money, jokes about drinking or past behaviour, pregnancy announcements, and anything the couple has not agreed to be public. Do not read from a phone if you can avoid it — the screen lights your face badly and the temptation to scroll is real. Practise out loud at least three times and have a printed card.

Quick answers

Who gives a wedding speech?

Traditionally the best man, maid of honour, parents and the couple, but there is no rule. Shorten the list before shortening the speeches.

How long should a best man speech be?

Three to five minutes is a safe ceiling. Anything over six loses the room regardless of how good the material is.

Should the speech be funny or sincere?

Both, in that order: one warm story, one light joke at your own expense, then a sincere close. Never make the couple the punchline.
