What the wedding video editor does

A full wedding video is long. Three hours of ceremony is not a thing you send to anybody. The clip people actually want is often forty seconds long, and the honest work is finding those forty seconds and then making them small enough to send.

This editor does three jobs and stops there. It cuts the video between an in point and an out point that you choose. It changes the frame size when the clip has to fit a phone screen or a feed. And it writes the result as MP4, WebM or MKV. Everything happens on your own machine, so there is no upload, no queue and no waiting for someone else's server.

The engine is mediabunny, which drives the WebCodecs encoder that is already built into your browser. That is why the ceiling here is the free memory in your device rather than an upload limit set by somebody else, and it is also why the browser you use matters.

What it does

  • Cuts a video to an in point and an out point, in seconds.
  • Sets the frame size to 1920 × 1080, 1080 × 1920, 1280 × 720 or 854 × 480.
  • Fits the frame either by letterboxing, so nothing is cut off, or by cropping to fill it.
  • Writes MP4, WebM or MKV and downloads one finished file.
  • Shows the length of the source, the length of the clip and the source frame size while you work.

What it does not do

  • No music, no subtitles, no speed change.
  • It does not join several clips into one.
  • No filters, no transitions, no text, no timeline.
  • No account, no cloud rendering, no watermark.

How to trim a wedding video

Three steps, and the middle one is the one that matters.

  1. Load the file. Drop it on the box, or click the box to browse. The video appears straight away and the length of the source shows under the player.
  2. Set the two points. Type the in point and the out point in seconds, or play the video and press the two buttons to take the in point and the out point from where you are right now.
  3. Pick the size and the format, then export. Choose a frame size, choose whether a smaller frame letterboxes or crops, choose MP4, WebM or MKV, and press the export button. One file downloads.

One thing is worth knowing before you start. If the clip is already in the format you asked for and you did not change the frame size, the editor copies the original video stream instead of re-encoding it. That is fast and it changes nothing in the picture. As soon as you change the size or the container, the video has to be re-encoded, and a re-encode always costs a little quality.

The in point snaps to the nearest keyframe before it. That is deliberate, because a keyframe is a complete picture, so the first frame of your clip is never half a picture. The clip can therefore start a moment before the second you typed.

Which format should you export for WhatsApp, Instagram and YouTube?

The format is the wrapper around the video and the audio. It is the first thing to get right, because it decides where the file plays at all.

For a phone, use MP4. convertbox.app recommends MP4 with H.264 for Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts, and feelthefile.com lists Instagram at up to 1080 × 1920 and 30 fps and YouTube at up to 1080p and 60 fps. The vertical preset in this editor is exactly 1080 × 1920.

This editor does not show you a bitrate slider. For what one is worth, justright.tools publishes a useful rule of thumb: about 1000 to 2000 kbps for standard definition, 2500 to 5000 kbps for HD and 8000 to 15000 kbps for Full HD, on a quality scale that runs from 0 for lossless to 51 for the worst quality, with a default of 23. Here the encoder picks a good quality for the frame size you asked for, and you do not have to think about it.

Frequently asked questions

Are my wedding videos uploaded to a server?

No. The file is read by your own browser, the clip is trimmed there and the export is written there. Nothing is sent to a server, no account is needed, and there is no watermark on the file you get.

What video formats does the editor read, and what can it export?

It reads MP4, MOV, WebM and MKV, which covers what a phone, a camera or a drone produces, and it exports MP4, WebM or MKV. MP4 is the safe choice for phones and WhatsApp, WebM makes the smallest files for the web, and MKV keeps every stream it can carry.

Will trimming or converting reduce the quality of my video?

Trimming a clip that is already in the format you want copies the original video stream, so nothing is re-encoded and the picture is untouched. Changing the size or the container re-encodes the video, and a re-encode always loses a little. Every converter makes that same trade.

Do I need to install anything or create an account?

No. There is no app to install, no account, no email and no watermark. The video encoder is fetched from this site the first time you export, and after that the browser keeps it in its own cache.

How long does the export take?

It depends on the job and on your machine. A short clip that only needs trimming is copied and finishes in seconds, while a long clip that has to be re-encoded to a new size is real work and takes minutes. The progress bar shows how far it has got. For comparison, convertbox.app says a 15 second clip converts in a few seconds and a clip near its 50 MB limit can take up to a minute.

Is there a file size limit?

There is no upload cap, because nothing is uploaded. The real ceiling is the free memory in your device, and a long high definition clip needs a lot of it. For comparison, convertbox.app publishes a 50 MB maximum, feelthefile.com publishes 250 MB per file, and clipzeal.com publishes 500 MB on a desktop.

Can I export it for WhatsApp, Instagram or YouTube?

Yes, that is what the size presets are for. convertbox.app recommends MP4 with H.264 at 1080 x 1920 for Reels, TikTok and YouTube Shorts, and feelthefile.com lists Instagram at up to 1080 x 1920 and 30 fps and YouTube at up to 1080p and 60 fps. Pick the vertical preset, choose Crop to fill, and export MP4.

Can I add music, subtitles or change the speed?

No, and this page does not pretend otherwise. It trims, resizes and converts. Music, subtitles, a speed change and joining several clips are all left out on purpose, so that the three things it does do are done properly and quickly.

Which browser do I need?

The export needs WebCodecs, the browser API that does the actual video work. Chrome and Edge have shipped it for years, Safari 16.4 and newer have it, and freevideogenerator.io lists Firefox as limited support. If the export button refuses to run, that is usually why.

Is the wedding video editor free?

Yes. It is free, with no account, no watermark and no limit on how many clips you cut. There is nothing to buy and nothing to upgrade to.
