Why a written list beats a friendly chat

The ranking US guides — A Practical Wedding, Plan In Love, Edel Ryan, Precious Moments Events — all make the same point: vendors are interviewed before contracts are signed, and the questions that matter are about deliverables, backups and cancellation terms, not the portfolio. A good vendor answers clearly and in writing; a vague one is a red flag.

Start every conversation with the same five

  1. Are you available on our date, and have you worked at our venue before?
  2. What exactly is included in this quote or package?
  3. Do you carry liability insurance and any required permits?
  4. What is the payment schedule, cancellation and refund policy?
  5. What is your backup plan if you, your equipment or a key supplier fails?

Red flags the ranked guides agree on

Vague or missing contracts; no insurance or business licence; slow or evasive communication; pressure to sign on the spot; no online presence or references; and refusal to show a full gallery or a full film rather than a highlight reel. Ask for two recent references and actually call them — the question to ask is not "were they good?" but "how did they handle something going wrong?"

Once vendors are booked, put their arrival times into the day-of timeline, their costs into the budget calculator, and their envelopes into the tip calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What should I ask a wedding venue before booking?

Ask what is included in the rental (tables, chairs, linens, lighting), how many hours it covers, the noise curfew and decor restrictions, the indoor rain backup, the alcohol policy, whether other events run simultaneously, and the cancellation terms.

What should I ask a wedding photographer?

Ask to see one complete wedding gallery rather than highlights, who will personally shoot your day, whether a second shooter is included, the turnaround time, whether you get full printing rights to high-resolution images, and the backup-equipment plan.

What are the biggest wedding vendor red flags?

A vague or missing contract, no liability insurance, slow or evasive replies, pressure to sign immediately, no reviews or references, and refusing to show full deliverables. Any one of these is worth walking away from.

Should I ask about cancellation and refund policies?

Always, and get the answer in the contract. Ask about the payment schedule, what happens if the vendor cannot fulfil the commitment, and whether the deposit is transferable to a new date.

How many vendors should I interview?

Two to three per category is a sensible shortlist for a US wedding. Use the same question list for each so you can compare answers directly rather than relying on who gave the best sales pitch.
