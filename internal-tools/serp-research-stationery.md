## 2. The facts the ranking pages agree on

### Piece sizes (the US industry default)

| piece | size | envelope |
|---|---|---|
| invitation | **5 × 7 in** (127 × 178 mm) | A7, 5.25 × 7.25 in |
| RSVP / reply card | **3.5 × 5 in** (A1) or 4.25 × 5.5 in (A2) | 4-bar / A2 |
| details card | 4.25 × 5.5 in (A2) | A2 |
| place card | 3.5 × 2 in | none — it sits on the table |
| menu card | 4.25 × 5.5 in | A2 |
| table number | 5 × 7 in | none |
| save the date | 5 × 7 in or 4 × 6 in | A7 |
| thank-you card | 3.5 × 5 in | 4-bar |
| poster / welcome sign | 18 × 24 in | none |

The invitation size is the one that is NOT a matter of taste: 5 × 7 is the
US default because it fits an A7 envelope and stays inside standard letter
postage. Every ranking page says so.

### Print resolution

300 DPI is the standard, and the pixel maths is quoted everywhere:
**5 × 7 in at 300 DPI = 1500 × 2100 px**. The guides also note that a
print shop wants **3 mm bleed** and **CMYK**, and that the letter page is
US Letter (8.5 × 11 in) with crop marks.

### Paper

* 80–110 lb cover (216–300 gsm) for the invitation; 65–80 lb for the RSVP
  and details cards.
* Over 110 lb "usually too thick for home printers" — check the printer spec.
* 80–100 gsm copier paper is what makes an invitation look cheap.

### Printer settings - the instruction every page repeats

**Print at 100% / "Actual Size", never "Fit to Page".** It appears on
placecard.us, apollostemplates.com, printablepress.com and eathealthy365.com,
and it is one of placecard.us's own FAQ questions. Two-sided printing
flips on the long edge unless the file says otherwise.

### Timing

* 6 weeks is the **absolute minimum**; **8–10 weeks is the standard**
  (paperlust: "arriving with guests 6–8 weeks"; bridalbybentley: "mail
  invitations eight to ten weeks before the wedding"; roomblocks: "six
  weeks is the absolute minimum, but eight to ten weeks is strongly
  preferred").
* Save the dates go out 6–8 months ahead.
* Destination weddings: 3 months.
* Print-ready lead time: 12–16 weeks before the wedding if a print shop
  is doing the run.

### Quantity and postage

One invitation per household, plus 10–15 % extra. A 100-guest wedding is
about 50 households, so about 60 invitations. Under one ounce = one
forever stamp; wax seals, ribbons or rigid stock make it non-machinable
(+$0.49); a square envelope is always non-machinable.

### Wording

The hosts' names, the couple's names, the date, the time of the ceremony,
the ceremony venue and its city, the reception venue or "reception to
follow", the dress code, and an RSVP request with a deadline. Everything
else — accommodations, parking, the website — goes on a **details card**,
not on the invitation.

### What the winners say about themselves

Every one of the top pages insists on the same two things:

1. **"It is a print-ready PDF, not a Canva link."** placecard.us spends a
   whole section on it: *"This page is a print-ready PDF download — not an
   online editor. If you need to drag-and-drop every text box, use Canva
   or a paid Etsy suite."*
2. **"Nothing is uploaded / no account."** apollostemplates.com: *"Every
   design and every download, the whole suite included, is free, with no
   watermark, no account and no email address."*

### The FAQ questions that rank

placecard.us's own FAQ block is the single best map of the intent:

* What is included in a wedding invitation suite?
* What size is a wedding invitation? · What size is an RSVP card?
* Can I print wedding invitations at home?
* **Should I print at 100% or fit to page?**
* What cardstock weight should I use?
* Is this an editable Canva template?
* Can I put every guest name on the invitation from Excel?
* Does this match place cards and table numbers?
* Is the PDF free for personal use?

## 3. What our page already had

Checked against the list above, the page built on 2026-11-10 already had:

* the six piece sizes and their pixel sizes (1500 × 2100 for the 5 × 7 at
  300 DPI — the same number every guide quotes),
* the 300 DPI decision and the reason the poster is 150 DPI,
* the paper weight ladder (100 lb standard, 110–120 lb heavier, 80–100 gsm
  copier paper = cheap),
* "print one sheet on cheap paper first" as a sacrificial proof,
* trim with a paper trimmer or a craft knife on a metal ruler, never
  scissors, plus the crop marks the PDF carries,
* clustering several cards on one sheet,
* the wording checklist (couple, hosts, date, time, venue, city,
  reception, dress code, RSVP) and the rule that the details card exists
  so the invitation does not get crowded,
* the quantity rule (one per household + 10–15 %),
* the comparison table against Canva / Greetings Island / a local print
  shop, including "Your wording leaves your device".

## 4. What was missing, and what changed

| gap | why it matters | change |
|---|---|---|
| **"Print at 100% / Actual Size, not fit to page" was nowhere on the page** | it is on four of the top ten results and it is one of the #1 result's own FAQ questions. It is the single most repeated instruction in this SERP, and a page that omits it sends the reader to the wrong printer setting and gets a 4×6 card off a 5×7 sheet | added to the printing section AND added as an FAQ question |
| **the "What's in the set" table had no envelope column** | every guide gives the size together with the envelope it fits — it is how the reader knows what to buy. The invitation is 5 × 7 *because* of A7 | an "Envelope" column was added to the table |
| **the send window was "six to eight weeks"** | the SERP consensus is 8–10 weeks, with 6 weeks as the absolute minimum. Our page said the minimum as if it were the norm | changed to "eight to ten weeks ... six weeks is the absolute minimum" |

Not changed, on purpose:

* **no price anywhere.** The pages that rank sell downloads and print
  services; we do not compete on price, we compete on "your wording never
  leaves your device" and "no account, no watermark".
* **no Canva-style editor.** The SERP makes the print-ready PDF a feature
  ("not a reflowing Canva page that shifts between printers"), and our
  export is a real PDF at real sizes, so this is the position to hold.
* **the 3.5 × 5 RSVP card stays.** The guides use both 3.5 × 5 and
  4.25 × 5.5; 3.5 × 5 is the USPS postcard minimum and mails at the
  postcard rate, which is the better default.
