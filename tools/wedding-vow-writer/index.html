Why vows are now the centre of the ceremony

The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study, based on 10,474 US couples married in 2025, found that 61% wrote their own vows — the highest rate ever recorded, up from 44% in its 2019 study. As ceremonies move away from a fixed religious script, the vows carry more of the emotional weight. That also makes them harder to write: a vow has to sound like one specific person speaking to one specific person, in front of everyone they love.

Ranked US guides from Zola, Joy and independent officiants converge on a 150–250 word target, which takes roughly one to two minutes to say aloud. The structure below is the four-part version used by most of them.

The four-part frame and its word budget

PartWhat it doesShare of a 200-word vow
1. OpenSay their actual name and one line about who they are~7%

4. CloseOne short line about the life ahead, then stop~10%

The promises are the part that separates a vow from a toast. A line with "I will" in it is a promise; a list of qualities is a speech. Officiants quoted in the top-ranking guides suggest three to six of them, and warn that vague ones are where guests switch off.

Read it aloud, slowly

The word counter above estimates speaking time at 150 words per minute (average US conversation pace) and at 100 wpm (a slow, nervous ceremony pace). Plan for the slower number: pauses, a breath, and a wobble or two will add time. Print the final draft on a card rather than reading it from a phone — several of the ranked guides call this out as the single most repeated practical tip.

A note on AI and vows

Zola's 2026 First Look Report, covering more than 11,500 couples, found 63% say AI has no place in writing vows. That is why this is a builder, not a ghostwriter: it supplies the frame, the prompts and the pacing, and you supply every sentence. Nothing is sent to a model or a server. If you want the traditional wording alongside your own, check with your officiant — Catholic, Hindu, Jewish and civil ceremonies all have different rules about what may be added.

What comes next

Once the vows are drafted, the other words on the day are the reception speeches. And when the day is over, those words become the audio spine of a cinematic wedding film — the reason so many couples choose to record them clearly.

Frequently asked questions

How long should wedding vows be?

Most US guides recommend 150–250 words, which takes about one to two minutes to read aloud. Read at a slower pace than feels natural — nerves speed you up, and a 200-word vow at 100 words per minute takes two minutes.

What is the basic structure of personal wedding vows?

Four parts: an opening that says your partner's name, one specific shared memory, three to six concrete promises, and a single closing line about the future. In a 200-word vow the story and promises take roughly 40% each.

How many couples write their own vows?

61% of US couples wrote personal or personalised vows according to The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study of 10,474 couples — the highest rate recorded, up from 44% in 2019.

Can I combine traditional vows with personal ones?

Often yes. A hybrid — a brief personal statement followed by the required traditional wording — is increasingly common and satisfies most liturgical requirements. Check with your officiant: Catholic ceremonies in particular restrict the approved formulations.

Does this tool use AI to write my vows?

No. Zola's 2026 First Look Report found 63% of couples believe AI has no place in writing vows. This builder only arranges sentences you type into a proven four-part structure, counts the words, and estimates speaking time. Nothing leaves your browser.

Should I read my vows from my phone?

Most officiants and ranked guides recommend a printed card instead. A phone screen reads as a script, glares in outdoor light, and is easy to lose in the moment. Use the Print / PDF button and trim the page to card size.
