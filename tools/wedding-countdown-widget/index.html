What couples actually do with a countdown

Ranked widget sites (Countz, Counterdown, WatchIsUp) describe the same set of uses: a share link in the family group chat, an iframe on the wedding website, a story card on Instagram or TikTok, and a full-screen display at the venue entrance on the day. The engagement-to-wedding gap in the US averages about 15 months, so a countdown is one of the few things that makes the date feel concrete early on.

The practical details that separate a good widget from a bad one are time zones (the event's local time, stored as UTC so daylight-saving changes do not shift it), a clean zero state, and no account requirement for guests who open the link.

Frequently asked questions

How do I add a countdown to my wedding website?

Generate it here, copy the iframe embed code, and paste it into an HTML or embed block. It works with WordPress, Wix, Squarespace, Carrd and Notion, and adapts to the available width.

Can guests see the countdown without an account?

Yes. The share link is public and read-only — anyone with the link can open it on a phone or computer. Do not put private information such as a home address in the message.

Does the countdown handle time zones and daylight saving?

The event time is interpreted in the venue's local time and stored as an instant, so a daylight-saving change between now and the wedding does not shift the day count. Set the ceremony time as your venue's local time.

What should the countdown show when the day arrives?

A clean zero state and a short arrival message — never negative days or a frozen 00:00:00 that keeps ticking backwards. This page switches to an arrival message at zero.

What milestones are worth sharing?

Six months, 100 days, one month and one week are the four that ranked guides suggest. Screenshot the story card at each one; it is an easy, low-effort way to keep guests excited and the date in their calendars.

Is anything uploaded to a server?

No. The date, names and style live in this page and in your browser's local storage. The share link encodes the settings in the URL, so there is no database and no account.
