When to use a wedding photo collage

Collages are one of the most quietly useful print and digital assets a wedding produces. A single well-designed layout can pull double duty as a save-the-date, a thank-you card insert, a photo album cover, a parent gift, and a recap post on Instagram. The trick is choosing a layout that fits the medium — Instagram punishes edge-of-face crops, printers hate uneven white borders, and parent albums want generous breathing room so the photos can breathe next to each other.

Save-the-date cards (mail or e‑card)

  1. 2 × 2 square at 1:1. Compact, prints cleanly on a 5×5 card, and the four-cell grid works as the "frame" around a central detail line.
  2. Horizontal strip at 16:9. Use a panoramic engagement shot as the anchor cell and surround it with three detail shots — rings, flowers, the venue.
  3. Vertical strip at 4:5. Tails a long, magazine-style save-the-date that scrolls well in Instagram DMs and still prints at 5×7 with a thin border.

Thank-you cards (and parent gift prints)

  1. 2 × 3 at 4:5. Six photos is the magic number for a thank-you card — enough variety without crowding. Portrait aspect ratio works for US 4×6 and 5×7 cards.
  2. 3 × 3 at 1:1. The classic nine-up grid is timeless. Equally readable as a 5×5 print or an Instagram post.
  3. 3 × 4 at 4:5. For the parent album insert — pack twelve of the day's most important shots into one sheet. Pair with our Add Text to Wedding Photo tool to label each cell.

Social media recap (Instagram, Facebook, WeChat)

  1. 3 × 3 at 1:1. The Instagram-friendly nine-grid. Square in the feed, square in a carousel slide.
  2. 2 × 3 at 4:5. The Instagram portrait six-grid — takes 60% more vertical space than a square in the algorithm's preview, so it tends to stop the scroll.
  3. Horizontal strip at 16:9. For Facebook cover photos, LinkedIn, or the header image in a wedding blog post. Wide format works for landscape cropping on the Instagram grid.

Why we made this in the browser

Most online collage makers upload your photos to a server, process them, and ship the result back. That's fine for most uses, but wedding photos are unusually private — couples often include family-only moments, behind-the-scenes candids, and pictures of guests who haven't consented to remote storage. We designed this tool to never touch a server: the Canvas API does the compositing in your browser tab, and the PNG lands on your device directly. No analytics on your photos, no training-data collection, no account creation.

The same approach let us ship fonts without font hosting concerns. Most online tools have to bundle their fonts in the page so they can be applied at render time — which can fail when a CDN is blocked, ad-blocker is aggressive, or font license restricts web embedding. We use Google Fonts' font-display: swap strategy: the canvas waits a brief moment for the font to load before drawing the caption, then renders. No broken text, no surprise fallback fonts in your finished collage.

Accessibility & what this tool is not

A collage is a visual artifact. The captioned version carries a single text string, which we render with a soft shadow on top of the background color so it remains readable against light or dark images. If you need a long block of text on each cell, use our Add Text tool on each individual photo first, then drop the labeled photos into this collage. We deliberately don't generate text-only collages — screen readers can't extract a story from a JPG, and caption-burned images exclude the visually impaired from your wedding's retelling. For a textual narrative use the original photo album plus a written card.

Step-by-step tutorial

  1. Pick your photos first. Open your favorites folder and choose 4, 6, 9, or 12 photos. Sort them roughly in the order you want them to appear — left-to-right, top-to-bottom.
  2. Drop them in. Drag-and-drop into the dropzone, or click to browse. The first preview thumbnails appear immediately so you can confirm orientation.
  3. Pick a layout. Click one of the six layout chips. The "best for" hint changes underneath — use it as a sanity check.
  4. Pick an aspect ratio. 1:1 for Instagram, 4:5 for portrait Instagram and most printers, 16:9 for desktop banners, 4:3 for traditional 6×4 prints.
  5. Tweak padding, background, and caption. Drag the padding slider. Pick a background color that contrasts your dominant photo tones. Type a single-line caption and pick a font.
  6. Build the collage. Click Build collage. The Canvas API composites each photo with cover-crop semantics — no stretched faces, no awkward side-zoom on landscape photos.
  7. Download. The Download PNG button stays available afterwards so you can rebuild with different settings and re-export.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos can I include in one collage?

2 to 12. The exact count per layout is: 2 × 2 = 4, 2 × 3 = 6, 3 × 3 = 9, 3 × 4 = 12, horizontal strip = 5, vertical strip = 5. If you add fewer than the layout expects, the remaining cells render as background color. If you add more, only the first n are used (and the file list shows which were excluded).

Will my photos be stretched or distorted?

No. Each photo is drawn with object-fit: cover semantics — centered, scaled so it fills the cell, with the overflow cropped symmetrically. Faces and bodies stay framed even when portrait and landscape shots are mixed in the same collage.

What format is the output?

A single PNG at print resolution. The canvas dimensions match the aspect ratio you chose — for example, 1:1 renders at 1500 × 1500 px, 4:5 at 1500 × 1875 px, 16:9 at 1920 × 1080 px, and 4:3 at 1600 × 1200 px. PNG is lossless, so the file is bigger than a JPG but the quality is identical to what you preview.

Does this work on iPhone or Android?

Yes. Both iOS Safari and modern Android Chrome support the Canvas API and Google Fonts. On mobile, tap the dropzone to open your camera roll.

Why a custom caption — can I add multiple lines?

Single-line captions stay readable when the collage is rendered small (Instagram grid thumbnail, e-card preview, paper print at 5×5). For longer captions or per-cell labels, use the Add Text to Wedding Photo tool on each individual photo first.

Are my photos uploaded?

No. Every pixel is composited in your browser via the Canvas API. There is no server. Disconnect your Wi-Fi after dropping the photos — the tool still works.
