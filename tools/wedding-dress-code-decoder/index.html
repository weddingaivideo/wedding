One ladder, ten labels

Vogue's guide to modern guest dress codes and the ranked US etiquette pages all describe the same spectrum, from white tie down to casual. The confusion comes from invented labels — "Romantic Garden Attire", "Elevated Casual", "Champagne Dreams" — which couples increasingly print on invitations. Emily Post's guidance is to map the invented phrase to the nearest standard rung, then calibrate for venue and time of day.

The rules that still hold in 2026

No white, ivory, cream or champagne — that remains the firmest rule in Western wedding etiquette, including "but it has tiny flowers on it". Avoid the bridal party's exact colour if you know it. Black is now fully acceptable at modern US and European weddings; the old mourning association has largely faded, though some families and faith communities still hold it. And "casual" at a wedding is still three levels above a couch outfit — jeans and sneakers only if the invitation literally says so.

Three calibration rules

  • Ceremony trumps reception. Dress for the most formal part of the day; you can shed a jacket later.
  • Venue is a hint. Ballroom means go up a rung; barn or beach means down — unless the invite says otherwise.
  • When in doubt, overdress slightly. Slightly overdressed always photographs better, and a 20-second text to the couple beats an evening of feeling wrong.

If you are the couple writing that line on the invitation, put the standard wording bottom-right on the invite or on your wedding website — one line saves fifty texts. For the wording itself, see the invitation maker.

Frequently asked questions

What does black tie optional actually mean?

The couple wants a black-tie look but will not force you to rent a tux. Men: a tuxedo if you own one, otherwise your darkest suit with a conservative tie. Women: a long gown, a formal midi or dressy separates. 'Optional' applies to the tux, not to dressing formally.

Can I wear black to a wedding?

Yes. Black is fully accepted at modern US and European weddings, especially cocktail and black-tie ones. Avoid it only at explicitly bright or festive daytime weddings, or add colour through accessories. Some families and faith communities still associate it with mourning.

What should a wedding guest never wear?

White, ivory, cream or anything close to it; the bridal party's exact colour; jeans and sneakers unless the invitation says casual; and anything attention-grabbing enough to pull focus from the couple.

What is the difference between formal, semi-formal and cocktail?

Formal sits near black-tie optional (dark suit, long or dressy midi dress). Cocktail is the most common US code (suit, knee-to-midi dress). Semi-formal is one rung down (blazer, dressy separates). When unsure, take the higher rung.

What should I wear to a beach or garden wedding?

Light fabrics — linen, chiffon, cotton. Women: a flowy midi or maxi with wedges or block heels (stilettos sink into grass and sand). Men: a light suit or blazer with an open collar, no tie, loafers.

What if the invitation uses an invented dress code?

Map the phrase to the nearest standard rung: 'Romantic Garden Attire' is garden-party semi-formal; 'Festive and Fun' is cocktail with colour. Then check the wedding website for a 'What to Wear' note and calibrate for venue and start time.
