Why add text to a wedding photo?

A great wedding photo carries the moment — the smile, the dress, the lighting. A small caption turns that photo into a keepsake: a save-the-date card your guests actually read, a thank-you note that lands, a slide in an anniversary reel. This tool exists for that small but powerful overlay.

The five captions couples actually need

  1. Save-the-date cards. "Save the Date · June 14, 2026" over an engagement or first-look photo. The headline font does the heavy lifting; keep it large and centered (top-center or bottom-center).
  2. Thank-you cards with a photo. A small line of gratitude ("Thank you for celebrating with us — Emma & Liam") in a script font, lower corner, white with a soft shadow.
  3. Anniversary posts for social. "Three years" or "Ten years" at 120–160px on a single hero shot. Script fonts (Great Vibes, Sacramento) read as romantic at any thumbnail size.
  4. Parent and grandparent albums. A date and a location ("Banff · September 3, 1994") helps older relatives place the photo in time. Use a classic serif — Playfair Display or Libre Baskerville — with the default shadow on.
  5. Slideshow opening titles. The first frame of a wedding photo slideshow often sets a date and a name. Render that frame here, drop it in, then move into the photo sequence.

Choosing the right font

Font choice carries half the meaning of a captioned photo. The 14 fonts in this tool are split into three families — and each family has a job.

  • Script (Great Vibes, Sacramento, Pacifico, Dancing Script) — romantic, handwritten feel. Best for invitations, thank-you cards, anniversary posts. Pair with a subtle shadow so it reads on any background.
  • Serif (Playfair Display, Cormorant Garamond, Bodoni Moda, DM Serif Display, Cinzel, Libre Baskerville, Lora, Abril Fatface) — elegant, balanced, traditional. Best for save-the-dates, formal announcements, parent albums, slideshow titles.
  • Sans (Montserrat, Inter) — modern, clean, minimal. Best for contemporary weddings, neutral captions, modern-minimalist albums.

When in doubt, the default Playfair Display is a safe, elegant serif that works on almost any photo. Switch to a script font for a more personal feel, or to a sans for a modern look.

Shadow, contrast, and legibility

The drop-shadow toggle adds a soft semi-transparent black offset behind the text. Default on, default 2px. It costs almost nothing in file size and dramatically improves legibility over busy or bright backgrounds — white text on a sunlit beach or a dark dress still reads cleanly with the shadow enabled.

If your photo has a soft, even background (a studio portrait, neutral wall), turn the shadow off and pick a color that already contrasts — dark wine #7A1F2B on cream, or pure white on dark.

Frequently asked questions

What if my caption is too long to fit?

The canvas measures the rendered text on every preview tick. If the primary line would overflow, the font is auto-shrunk in 2px steps down to a 24px minimum. If 24px is still too wide, the canvas clips at the edges — shorten the caption or split it onto two with the secondary text field.

Why is PNG the default format?

PNG keeps the original photo pixels untouched where the text isn't drawn and supports transparency — useful if you plan to drop the captioned image into a layout later. JPG is smaller for direct sharing; switch the format dropdown to JPG and the same render uses JPG instead.

Can I caption multiple photos at once?

Yes. Drop a batch and the same text settings apply to every photo. Each row gets its own Download button; Save all downloads the whole batch with sequential delays to avoid browser blocking.

Will the text work in the AI Wedding Video Generator?

The captioned PNG is exactly the format the AI Wedding Video Generator reads as a title frame. Use this tool to render the first captioned photo, then upload the full photo set to the generator for a 60–90 second film.

Does the text look pixel-perfect when printed?

At 300 DPI, a 56px primary caption on a 2048px-wide photo renders at roughly 41pt — readable from arm's length on a 5×7 print. For larger prints (8×10 and up), bump the primary size to 80–100px before rendering.
