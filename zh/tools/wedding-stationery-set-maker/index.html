What to put on a wedding invitation

The invitation is the first formal thing your guests read about the wedding, and it carries the logistics. A complete invitation usually has:

  • The names of the couple.
  • The names of the hosts, if your families are hosting — this set prints the family line for you.
  • The date and the time of the ceremony.
  • The ceremony venue and its city.
  • The reception venue, or a line saying the reception follows the ceremony.
  • The dress code, if you have one.
  • An RSVP request with a deadline and a way to reply.

Anything further - accommodations, parking directions, a link to the wedding website - usually belongs on its own details card instead of crowding the invitation. That is exactly why the set gives you an RSVP card rather than cramming one more paragraph onto the front page.

How to print your stationery set

The file is the easy part. The paper is what people actually feel.

  • Print on cardstock, never on copy paper. 100 lb cardstock is the standard for invitations; 110 to 120 lb reads as noticeably more considered. 80 to 100 gsm copier paper is what makes a beautiful invitation look cheap.
  • Set the printer to its best mode - "High" or "Best" rather than "Standard" - so the edges are crisp and the colour is deep.
  • Print at 100% or "Actual Size", never "Fit to Page" A scaled sheet turns a 5 × 7 invitation into a different size, the trim guides no longer match the paper, and a 4 × 6 card comes out of a 5 × 7 envelope.
  • Print one sheet on cheap paper first. A sacrificial test print catches the two mistakes that ruin a batch: the page is not centred, and the colour is darker or lighter than the design intends.
  • Trim with a paper trimmer or a craft knife on a metal ruler. Scissors leave a ragged edge; a sliding blade gives a straight one. The PDF has trim guides and crop marks for exactly this.
  • Cluster the cards on one sheet - two or four of a size per sheet - and you cut the paper waste and the trimming time at the same time.

For contrast: a bespoke design from a stationer takes weeks to come back, which is why so many couples print their own. The set is free and the paper is the only part you buy.

When to send wedding invitations

Eight to ten weeks before the wedding for a local wedding - six weeks is the absolute minimum, long enough for guests to book a hotel, short enough that it does not feel like an announcement. For a destination wedding, three months ahead, because people need to arrange travel and accommodation. Save the dates go out separately, six to eight months ahead.

On quantity: one invitation per household, plus 10 to 15 percent extra for late additions, for mail that goes astray, and for the keepsake that everyone asks for afterwards. A 100 guest wedding is about 50 households, so roughly 60 invitations.

Frequently asked questions

What information should be on a wedding invitation?

The couple's names, the hosts' names if they are hosting, the date and time of the ceremony, the ceremony venue, the reception venue or a note that the reception follows the ceremony, the dress code if there is one, and an RSVP request with a deadline. Everything else - accommodations, parking, the website - usually goes on the separate details card.

What size should a wedding invitation be?

5 × 7 inch is the standard in the United States, and it fits a standard A7 envelope. 4.25 × 5.5 inch is the smaller, more casual size and also fits A7 when folded. The set uses 5 × 7 for the invitation, 3.5 × 5 for the RSVP card, 3.5 × 2 for the place and table cards, 4.25 × 5.5 for the menu and 18 × 24 for the poster.

How many invitations should I order?

One per household, plus 10 to 15 percent extra for late additions, mail that goes astray and keepsakes. For a 100 guest wedding - around 50 households - that means about 60 invitations.

When should I send wedding invitations?

Eight to ten weeks before a local wedding - six weeks is the absolute minimum - and three months ahead for a destination wedding so guests can book travel. Save the dates go out on their own, six to eight months ahead.

Can I print wedding invitations at home?

Yes. The set exports as one PDF with every page at its real print size and 300 DPI, so a home printer, a local print shop and an online print service all receive the same file. Print on cardstock rather than copy paper.

Should I print at 100% or fit to page?

At 100%, or on the setting your printer calls "Actual size". "Fit to page" rescales the sheet to whatever paper is in the tray, so the invitation comes out the wrong size and the crop marks no longer match the cut.

What paper should I print on?

Cardstock - 100 lb for invitations, 110 to 120 lb for a heavier feel - rather than 80 to 100 gsm copier paper. Matte finish suits a romantic or natural design, glossy suits a photo-heavy one. Print one sheet on cheap paper first to check the alignment and colour.

Do I have to crop and trim the pages myself?

You trim, but nothing is left to guess: the pieces come at their finished sizes with trim guides and crop marks, and the invitation carries a 0.25 inch safe area so no important text lands in the cut.

Is it free? Do I need an account?

Yes to both. No account, no email, no watermark, and no limit on how many sets you make. Everything runs inside your browser.

Do my wedding details leave my device?

No. The pieces are drawn on a canvas in your browser and the PDF is assembled there as well. The names, dates and venues you type are never sent to a server.

Can I change the wording on each piece?

Yes. The wording is typed into the form rather than baked into the artwork, so you can add "reception to follow", a dress code, a different RSVP deadline, or leave a field empty and it simply does not print.
