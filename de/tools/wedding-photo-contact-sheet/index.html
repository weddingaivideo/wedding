What is a contact sheet?

A contact sheet is a single sheet of paper that shows an entire shoot as small numbered thumbnails. You can see the whole batch at a glance, mark the pictures you want, and hand the sheet to somebody else to choose from. Photographers call it a contact sheet; when the sheet exists so a client can pick their favourite pictures, it is also called a proof sheet.

The number under each thumbnail is the whole point. A couple who has just seen 800 photographs replies with a list - number 14 and number 22 - and the photographer goes straight to those pictures instead of describing them in words. The file name underneath does the same job from the other side: it points back to the original file on the hard drive.

Never cropped by default

Every photo is fitted inside its cell and nothing is cut off. A contact sheet exists to check framing, so a cropped thumbnail hides exactly the mistake you are looking for - a head cut off at the top, a person half out of the picture. Lightroom calls this the Fit setting. If you would rather have a grid of identical rectangles, switch the fit mode to Crop to fill and the overflow is trimmed instead.

Filenames and numbers

Each thumbnail carries a number and the original file name underneath it. The numbers run continuously across every page, so page two starts where page one stopped instead of restarting at one. That matters as soon as a sheet goes to two people at the same time: both write on the same sheet and you still know which picture they mean.

How to build a contact sheet

There are two ways to do this. The first is Adobe Lightroom Classic, and if you already use it this is a two minute job. Open the Print module - Alt+Ctrl+6 on Windows, ALT+CMD+6 on Mac - pick a layout from the Template Browser, set the Page Grid and the Cell Spacing, and turn on Photo Info with Sequence and Filename so the picture number and the file name are printed under every thumbnail. A 5 × 8 template is a common starting point, and the usual margin is about 0.25 inch.

One thing to know about Lightroom Classic: it has no built-in way to save a PDF straight out of the Print module. Photographers still print to PDF from the system dialog, or export the sheet as an image, which is why a separate contact sheet maker is still worth having open next to it.

The arithmetic is simple once you see it. A4 is 210 mm wide, and with a 10 mm margin on each side that leaves 210 − (2 × 10) = 190 mm of usable width. Divide that by four columns and take off the gaps, and each cell is about 44 mm wide. A 3:2 cell that size fits six rows down the page, so 4 × 6 = 24 photos on one A4 sheet.

Who uses a photo contact sheet?

  • A photographer sending a proof sheet to a client. Nobody can email 800 previews. The sheet is the preview - 24 to 40 photos per page, numbered, printed or emailed - so the couple marks the ones that go in the album.
  • The couple choosing their album pictures. If your photographer sends you a sheet, you can point at the numbers instead of describing them, and you can both write on the same sheet before you send it back.
  • Anyone ordering prints. The same method as an old film sleeve: pick the picture numbers you want enlarged, and the print shop prints exactly those pictures.
  • A print shop or an album company. Both ask for picture numbers, and both would rather have a numbered sheet than a paragraph of descriptions.

Print tips for proof sheets

  • Print at 100 percent, not scaled to fit. The PDF page is the real sheet size, so choose Actual size. Fit to page shrinks the sheet slightly, and then the printed numbers stop lining up with the photos.
  • Use plain paper or matte cardstock. Glossy photo paper throws reflections across the thumbnails, and a reflective sheet is much harder to write on. Cardstock is also sturdier to handle and easier to put in an envelope.
  • Print one test page first. One sheet on scrap paper catches a wrong paper size and a colour setting your printer changed without telling you.
  • Use a pencil, not a pen. People change their mind, and pencil comes off the page.
  • Write the number, not the description. The whole reason the number is printed under the photo is so nobody has to describe a face.

Printing and colour caveats

One honest limitation first: this is an image proof, not a colour-managed print guarantee. The sheet is drawn by a browser canvas, and the browser canvas specification records 96 DPI and browser RGB, so the file is not an ICC, CMYK or PDF/X prepress file. The output tells you which picture to pick - it does not promise that the printed colours will match your screen exactly.

That is the right tool for the job. A contact sheet is a selection aid: the question is always which photograph, not what shade of blue the dress was. If you need a colour-accurate proof before an expensive print, ask the print shop for a colour-managed proof of the final image and judge the colour there, not on the contact sheet.

Two more practical notes. The source files are never modified - the tool only reads them. And if you need the photographs themselves to be smaller before you email the sheet, Compress Wedding Photos does that first, and HEIC to JPG converts iPhone files before you add them.

Frequently asked questions

What is a contact sheet?

A contact sheet is one sheet of paper that shows an entire shoot as small numbered thumbnails, so you can review a whole batch at a glance instead of opening file after file. When the sheet exists so a client can pick their favourite pictures, it is also called a proof sheet.

How many photos fit on one page?

It depends on the paper size and the number of columns. On A4 portrait, four columns of 3:2 thumbnails fit 24 photos on one page, three columns fit 15, five columns fit about 40 and six columns fit about 54. The counter in the tool shows the exact count for your page size and column count before you export, so you never have to count by hand.

Should I crop the thumbnails or fit the whole photo?

Fit the whole photo. A contact sheet exists to check framing, so cutting a thumb off the edge hides exactly the mistake you are looking for. This tool fits every photo inside its cell and never crops, which is the same idea as the Fit setting in Lightroom. Crop to fill is available if you would rather have a grid of identical rectangles.

Can I print filenames and numbers under each thumbnail?

Yes, and both are on by default. Every thumbnail carries its number and its file name underneath, so a reply of number 14 and number 22 can always be matched back to a file. You can turn the numbers off, the file names off, or both off if you only want the pictures.

Can I download the contact sheet as a PDF?

Yes. The whole batch is exported as one multi-page PDF, not one file per page, so 60 photos come out as a single document with three pages. Each page keeps the real paper size, so printing at 100 percent gives you the A4, US Letter or 8x10 inch sheet you chose.

What page sizes and print resolution are supported?

Three sheet sizes: A4 at 210 by 297 mm, US Letter at 8.5 by 11 inch which is 215.9 by 279.4 mm, and 8 by 10 inch, each in portrait or landscape. Two resolutions: 300 DPI for printing and 150 DPI for emailing or reading on a tablet. At 300 DPI an A4 page is 2481 by 3507 pixels.

What resolution should I use for printing?

Use 300 DPI when the sheet goes to a printer. It is the resolution print shops expect, and it turns an A4 page into a 2481 by 3507 pixel image. Use 150 DPI when the PDF is only going to be emailed or opened on a tablet, because the file is four times smaller and the thumbnails still look sharp on screen.

Can I make a contact sheet from HEIC photos?

Yes. HEIC files from an iPhone are decoded inside your own browser and never uploaded. JPG, PNG and WebP work everywhere. Camera RAW files are not something a browser can open, so export those to JPG first.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Your photos are read and drawn by your own browser, and the PDF is assembled there as well. Nothing is sent to a server, no account is needed, and there is no watermark on the sheet.

Is the proof sheet maker free?

Yes. The contact sheet maker is free, with no account, no watermark and no limit on how many sheets you make. The paper you print it on is the only thing you pay for, and that is the same for every tool on this site.
